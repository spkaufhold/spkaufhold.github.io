Stephan P. Kaufhold

I am a cognitive and behavioral scientist studying how social relationships, experience, and environments shape thinking and behavior. My research primarily focuses on primates and I use observational, experimental, and computational methods.

Currently, I am a researcher in the Department of Cognitive Science at UC San Diego, where I work in the Comparative Cognition Laboratory of Dr. Federico Rossano.

Research Interests

My research asks how social relationships, experience, and environments shape learning, decision-making, and cooperation. I study primate behavior and cognition from a comparative perspective to better understand the evolution of human thinking.

I work primarily with sanctuary-living primates (including macaques, gibbons, orangutans, and chimpanzees) in semi-naturalistic environments, using non-invasive research methods. I also study coordination in human–dog teams and how built environments shape human cognition and behavior.

Some of my research questions include:

  • How do social relationships and group cultures shape learning, cooperation, and competition?
  • What is the role of space and distance in navigating social interactions?
  • How can individuals of different species coordinate across different perceptual worlds?
  • What roles do experience and disease exposure play in pathogen avoidance?
  • How do built environments shape thinking and behavior?

Current Projects

Currently, I am conducting several research projects exploring social tolerance, inhibitory control, pathogen sensitivity, and social learning in macaques at the Born Free USA Primate Sanctuary in Southern Texas. For example, I am collaborating with Jack Terwilliger to develop and implement custom computer vision algorithms that accurately track macaques’ proximity and trajectories using real-world coordinates during social tolerance tests.

Beyond primates, I am also interested in how individuals from different species can work together across very different perceptual worlds. Together with Jack Terwilliger, Samantha Kefer and Federico Rossano, I examine how professional detection teams coordinate during searches in realistic environments, combining computer vision tracking with behavioral coding.

I am also interested in how the environments we build shape our thinking and behavior. I contribute to the Cognitive Neuroscience for Architecture project by developing computer vision tools and large image datasets that support this research in David Kirsh’s XR Lab at UC San Diego.

Background

I completed my PhD in Cognitive Science at UC San Diego, where I studied how primates cooperate and compete over resources. Across four empirical projects, I found that their strategies vary with social relationships, environmental conditions, and the interaction between them.

I previously earned a BSc and MSc in Psychology at the University of Innsbruck in Austria and interned and worked at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig, Germany. During my PhD, I also completed the Specialization in Anthropogeny at the Center for Academic Research & Training in Anthropogeny (CARTA).
